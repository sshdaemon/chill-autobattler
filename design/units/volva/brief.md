# Völva

## Concept
A Norse seeress who travels with the Varangians: a woman in a dark hooded
cloak with an iron staff, strings of amber and bone beads, and a pouch at her
belt. Seeresses like her are known from graves (Fyrkat, Birka, Oseberg), where
iron staffs with a cage-like knob near the top were buried with them, and from
the sagas, where the völva foretells and curses. Her place on a battlefield is
only literary, so she is the least historical unit; keep her grounded.

## Role on the battlefield
Special (caster): she weakens and frightens the enemy's front line.

- Holds back until an enemy comes within 10 m or an ally is attacked, picks
  the **strongest** enemy (highest damage), then stands her ground and
  curses. (The plan had her keep 7 m and fall back below 40%; balancing
  dropped both, see balance.md.) Slip away gets her out of melee at a run.
- Her attack is a curse at 7 m: a jab of the staff and dark smoke on the
  target, not a projectile spell.
- **Strong against** melee ×1.3: massed melee walking at her is what she is
  for (the roster triangle: specials beat massed melee).
- **What beats her:** ranged units that outrange her (archers, strilets,
  bowmen) and other casters; anything that reaches her before her curses add up.

## How it should feel
Still, cold and ominous. Dark undyed and blue wool, a hood up, the iron staff
held upright, the silhouette long and narrow. Her powers read as ritual and
dread: a staff gesture, a pointing hand, dark smoke clinging to the cursed,
a grey pulse when she drives enemies back. Never glowing bolts, fire or
lightning.

## Skill ideas
1. **Curse:** her hits make the target take more damage for a few seconds.
2. **Dread:** every so often, enemies close to her flee from her briefly.
3. **Foretelling:** allies near her see the blow coming and dodge some of them.
4. **Slip away:** when melee gets close, she gets away from it.
5. **Staff of the seeress:** she is hardier than she looks.

## Avoid
- Fantasy-witch clichés: pointed hats, crystal balls, cauldrons.
- Fireballs, lightning, glowing orbs, sparkles; anything that reads as a mage.
- Religion as a joke or a villain trait.
- Horned helmets or other 19th-century "Viking" imagery.
