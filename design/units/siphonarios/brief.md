# Siphonarios

## Concept
The Roman (Byzantine) Greek fire operator. Leo VI's Taktika names hand siphons
(*cheirosiphona*) for use on land behind the shields; Greek fire itself was
mostly a naval weapon and its recipe is lost. He is a soldier in a padded coat
(*kavadion*) and a felt cap, with a short bronze siphon held in both hands and
clay fire pots slung at his belt. The weapon is technical and dangerous, not
magical: a hiss, a short roaring jet of sticky flame, burning pools on the
ground and black smoke.

## Role on the battlefield
Special: short-range area damage that punishes massed melee.

- Waits behind his own front line (*hold*, stays near melee) until enemies come
  within about 6 m, then jets everyone in a 60° cone up to 5 m in front of him.
- Keeps about 3 m from melee and is fragile up close only if he is caught alone.
- **Strong against** melee ×1.35: a pack of swordsmen or a shield wall walking
  into the jet is what he is for (the roster triangle: specials beat massed melee).
- **What beats him:** anything with range. Archers, bowmen, mages and the völva
  pick him off from outside 5 m; he has no answer but his fire pots.

## How it should feel
Disciplined, workmanlike, a little frightening. Dark-blue or green padded coat,
bronze and clay, soot. The siphon is held low at the hip and swung toward the
enemy; the jet reads as a wide fan of orange flame and smoke, and leaves the
ground burning for a few seconds. Fire pots are lobbed overhand and burst in
flame.

## Skill ideas
1. **Burning ground:** the jet leaves fire on the ground that burns anyone who stands in it.
2. **Fire pots:** every so often he lobs a clay pot of fire at a target out of jet reach; it splashes.
3. **Pressure:** a longer jet.
4. **Padded coat:** tougher.
5. **Fear of fire:** melee hit by the jet falters and slows.

## Avoid
- Anything that reads as a fire mage: glowing orbs, fireballs from the hands, robes, wizard hats.
- Dragons, flamethrower tanks, modern or steampunk imagery.
- Imperial purple on soldiers (it belongs only to an emperor's banner).
