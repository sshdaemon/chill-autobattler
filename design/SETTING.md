# Setting: the Dnipro road (Kyivan Rus, Varangians, Byzantium)

Status: direction agreed (November 2026), nothing built yet. This is the
general lore and look guide that the unit briefs in `design/units/` will follow.
It changes nothing in the rules on its own (see "How it maps to the game").

## The idea

The game is set in the 10th–11th century, the century of the "route from the
Varangians to the Greeks": the river road from the Baltic down the Dnipro,
through Kyiv, across the Black Sea to Constantinople. Three powers meet on it
and fight each other in every combination: as rivals, as allies and as
mercenaries.

**Era:** 941 (Igor's fleet burned by Greek fire) to 1043 (the last Rus–Byzantine
war). The core is the reigns of Volodymyr the Great and Yaroslav the Wise.

**Tone:** grounded and historical, a chronicle you can play. These are real
cultures with living heirs, so the game respects them: no caricature, and no
later imagery passed off as this era.

**Scale:** small war-bands and skirmishes (five against five) on river banks,
portage paths, forest edges and steppe borders, not sieges or great battles.

## Timeline anchors

The events that justify every matchup and give flavour to maps and texts:

- **907, Oleg's campaign** on Constantinople and the treaty that follows. The
  shield nailed to the city gate is chronicle legend.
- **941, Igor's attack**: the Rus fleet destroyed by Greek fire. 944: a new
  treaty.
- **957 (or 946), Olga's visit** to Constantinople and her baptism.
- **970–971, Sviatoslav in Bulgaria** against Emperor John I Tzimiskes, ending
  with the siege of Dorostolon. Leo the Deacon describes Sviatoslav in person.
- **988, Volodymyr's baptism** and marriage to Anna Porphyrogenita. Volodymyr
  sends about 6,000 Norse warriors to Emperor Basil II, the start of the
  Varangian Guard.
- **1015–1019, the succession war** after Volodymyr's death. Yaroslav wins it
  with hired Varangians.
- **1043, the last war** with Byzantium. Greek fire again destroys a Rus fleet.

## The three factions

Each faction has three units: a melee core, a ranged unit and a special unit
(today's warrior, archer and mage slots).

### Kyivan Rus: the Prince's druzhyna

The druzhyna is the prince's retinue: professional warriors who live, eat and
ride with him. Rus culture of this time mixes Slavic, Norse and steppe
influences and, after 988, Byzantine Christianity.

| Unit | Who | Look (historical basis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Druzhynnyk** (melee) | Prince's retainer | Mail shirt; conical helmet with a mail neck guard (the Chernihiv-type helmets); sword of Frankish type or a small battle axe; spear. Round shields in the 10th century, almond (teardrop) shields from the 11th. |
| **Strilets** (ranged) | Archer of the druzhyna or town levy | Composite recurved bow of steppe type (finds in Rus burials); quiver on the belt; padded or leather coat; fur-trimmed cap. |
| **Huslyar** (special) | Court singer with the husli | Long caftan, belt, cap; the husli (a lap harp) on a strap. Basis: Boyan, the singer of "earlier times" remembered in *The Tale of Igor's Campaign*; husli finds from the Novgorod excavations; songs at the prince's feasts. |

- **Colours:** crimson shields (the "crimson shields" of the Rus are a fixed
  phrase in the old texts), undyed linen, ochre, brown wool, silvered belt fittings.
- **Feel:** steady, well-armoured, together. The huslyar makes them stronger as
  a group.

### The Varangians

Norse warriors and traders of the eastern road: some settled in Rus, some
crewed river boats, some went on to serve the emperor. "Varangian" (Greek
*Varangoi*, Old East Slavic *variahy*) is what the Rus and Greeks called them.

| Unit | Who | Look (historical basis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Huskarl** (melee) | Sworn household warrior | Round painted wooden shield with an iron boss; Dane axe (a long two-handed axe, late 10th–11th century) or sword; mail for the wealthy, wool tunic otherwise; rounded or spangenhelm helmet (the Gjermundbu find). |
| **Bowman** (ranged) | Hunter-warrior | Long self bow of yew or elm (the Hedeby bow); light kit; spears for throwing as a skill. |
| **Völva** (special) | Seeress | Staff (iron staffs are found in seeresses' graves), hooded cloak, amulets, pouch. Basis: graves like those at Fyrkat and Birka and the sagas. |

- **Colours:** shield paint in yellow, black, red and white (the Gokstad
  shields); iron grey; undyed and blue wool; bone and amber.
- **Feel:** hard-hitting and reckless: high damage, lighter armour.
- **Accuracy note:** the völva is the least historical unit. Seeresses are well
  attested, but their place on a battlefield is only literary. Her powers
  should read as ritual and fear (curses, foretelling), never as fireballs.

### Byzantium: the Romans

The Eastern Roman Empire. Its people called themselves Romans (*Rhomaioi*);
"Byzantine" is a modern word. It had the best-organised army of its time, with
written manuals for everything.

| Unit | Who | Look (historical basis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Skoutatos** (melee) | Heavy infantryman | Long oval or large round shield (*skoutarion*); spear (*kontarion*) and sword (*spathion*); padded coat (*kavadion*), lamellar or mail for the front ranks; simple domed helmet. Basis: 10th-century military manuals, the Taktika of Leo VI and the *Praecepta militaria*. |
| **Toxotes** (ranged) | Archer | Composite bow; light padded coat; quiver and bow case. The *solenarion* (a guide tube for shooting short darts), described in the manuals, can be a skill. |
| **Siphonarios** (special) | Greek fire operator | Bronze hand siphon (hand siphons are named in Leo VI's Taktika); clay fire pots; padded coat. Greek fire was mostly a naval weapon and its recipe is lost; the hand version is real but rarely documented. |

- **Colours:** red and white with the cross and Chi-Rho on shields and banners;
  bronze; dark-blue and green coats. Purple is imperial and very rare: only an
  emperor's banner, never on soldiers.
- **Feel:** disciplined and technical: good armour, order, fire.

## Unit roles side by side

| Slot | Rus | Varangians | Romans |
|---|---|---|---|
| Melee | Druzhynnyk: balanced, armoured | Huskarl: big hits, less armour | Skoutatos: shield wall, slower |
| Ranged | Strilets: composite bow, mobile | Bowman: long bow, javelins | Toxotes: composite bow, darts |
| Special | Huslyar: songs that strengthen allies | Völva: curses and fear | Siphonarios: short-range fire |

## How it maps to the game

What stays the same: five against five, the run, veterans and skills, the
Blue and Red sides.

There are two possible steps:

1. **Looks only (no rule change).** Each faction is a set of looks for today's
   three classes. A team picks a faction and its units wear it. The druzhynnyk,
   huskarl and skoutatos all play as today's warrior. Units' displayed names and
   skill names change; the rules don't.
2. **Factions with their own rules (a rule change).** Each faction gets its own
   stats and skills.

The problem with step 1 is the special slot. Today's mage fires magic bolts,
which fits only the siphonarios (Greek fire at 7 m range, strong against
massed melee). A huslyar or völva shooting bolts would break "stay historical".
So the special slot needs step 2 and new effect kinds:

- **Huslyar:** an aura (allies nearby attack faster or take less damage) and a
  war song (the next charge hits harder). No damage of their own, or very little.
- **Völva:** fear (enemies nearby hesitate or retreat briefly) and a curse
  (a target takes more damage).
- **Siphonarios:** cone or splash fire, burning ground.

The suggested order: rename and restyle the melee and ranged units of the three
factions first (looks only), then add the new effect kinds and the three
special units one by one, each a balanced roster change.

## Naming and spelling

- **Kyiv, Dnipro, Chernihiv, Novgorod;** Ukrainian transliteration for Rus
  terms (druzhyna, druzhynnyk, kniaz, husli, huslyar, strilets).
- **Rus** or **Kyivan Rus**, never "Russia" or "Russian": the modern names
  belong to a much later state.
- **Varangians** for the Norse of the eastern road; "Vikings" only for raiders
  in the west.
- **Romans** in-game (with "Byzantium" in menus if clearer); Constantinople,
  never "Istanbul".

## Avoid (anachronisms and clichés)

- Horned helmets, plate armour, fur bikinis, giant fantasy weapons.
- Later Muscovite or Russian-imperial imagery: onion domes, kokoshnik headdresses,
  bardiches (long axes), 16th-century streltsy uniforms, double-headed eagles on
  Rus kit.
- Cossack imagery (17th century and later).
- Byzantine soldiers in purple, gold everywhere, or Crusader-style kit
  (Crusades start in 1096).
- Spell effects that read as fantasy magic (fireballs, lightning) for the
  huslyar or the völva.
- Religion used as a joke or a villain trait for any side.

## Sources to start from

Primary texts (in modern translation):
- *The Tale of Bygone Years* (the Primary Chronicle): Oleg, Igor, Olga,
  Sviatoslav, Volodymyr, Yaroslav.
- Constantine VII, *De Administrando Imperio*: the Dnipro rapids, with their
  Norse and Slavic names, and the Rus' yearly trip to Constantinople.
- Leo the Deacon, *History*: Sviatoslav and the war of 970–971.
- Leo VI, *Taktika*; Nikephoros II, *Praecepta militaria*: the Roman army.
- Ibn Fadlan's account of the Rus on the Volga (922).
- *The Tale of Igor's Campaign* (12th century): Boyan, the crimson shields.
- Icelandic sagas and skaldic verse, read with care: written down centuries later.

Archaeology: Gnezdovo, Shestovytsia, the Chorna Mohyla burial in Chernihiv,
Birka and Hedeby, the Novgorod excavations, the Gokstad and Gjermundbu finds.
